Theme values · --caution
Candidates, on their own card backgrounds
Each row is that theme's real card surface. Gold and wild are what the theme already spends in this register — the two things a caution badge can collide with. A is the obvious amber; B is the recommendation.
First: the three values already in the design are wrong
Doing this pass properly caught it. In Gilded the drafted caution was oklch(80% 0.13 78) and the theme's --gold is oklch(76% 0.14 75) — four points of lightness apart, same chroma, three degrees of hue. It is gold. Parchment was the same mistake (48% 0.13 62 against a gold of 45% 0.13 55), and Moonlit's sat two points off that theme's --acc-wild. A warning badge painted in the colour the whole interface already uses for decoration doesn't read as a warning.
The amber register is occupied in nearly every theme — that is what gold is here. So caution moves to orange: the hue between the theme's own red and its own amber. It reads as an escalation (amber → orange → red), and it is the one warm band no theme had spent.
Dark themes — one rule
oklch(from var(--neg) calc(l + 0.12) calc(c + 0.02) calc(h + 25)) — twelve points lighter than the theme's red, slightly hotter, and rotated a quarter of the way toward amber. Six themes take it as written; the values below are that formula resolved.

Light themes — the rule inverts, so these are hand-picked
On a near-white page lighter means less legible — the formula's l + 0.12 walks caution toward the background instead of away from it. Look at each A below: they are all washed out. So the four light themes take values rather than a rule.
